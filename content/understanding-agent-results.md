# What an AI agent's result is actually telling you

A useful agent result answers what was found, what was allowed, and what happened. Those are separate questions. If they are collapsed into one cheerful “done,” it becomes difficult to know whether the task ended with research, a blocked action, or a completed transaction.

The [Veyro demo](https://tryveyro.casa) includes a decision area and can show candidate and receipt information. Read those parts together. A candidate name alone is not a purchase confirmation.

## A candidate means something was selected

The agent may have found an option that matches its interpretation of your request. Look for the reason, source, and asset identity. The result is more useful when you can connect the selection to the words you typed.

If the explanation is based on a prepared example, that is different from live research. Veyro's product page says those sources are labeled separately. Treat a demonstration as a demonstration rather than assuming it reflects current X discussion.

See [searching X with an agent](/guides/search-x-with-an-agent) for the evidence to look for before treating a candidate as more than a lead.

## Allowed means a check passed

An “allow” decision can indicate that a proposed action met the relevant spending rules at a particular stage. It should not be read as a judgment that the selected token is legitimate, reasonably priced, or likely to produce a return.

It also should not be confused with final execution. A permitted action can still encounter a network problem or another failure before completion. The actual status and transaction evidence determine what happened next.

This distinction is not a technicality. If you read permission as completion, you may believe you own an asset that was never acquired. If you read permission as an endorsement, you may give the result more authority than it deserves.

## Denied can be the right outcome

A denial means the proposed action did not satisfy a condition the system checked. It may exceed the available budget, use an unsupported route, or fall outside active permission. The explanation should identify the relevant reason in plain language.

Do not immediately increase the amount just to make the message disappear. First ask whether the proposed action is still the one you wanted. A boundary is useful precisely because it can stop a task from proceeding.

Our guide to [why an agent stops](/guides/why-an-agent-stops) walks through the difference between a permission issue, missing information, and an unresolved execution result.

## Pending and unknown need patience, not a second purchase

When a transaction has been submitted, the interface may need time to establish its outcome. A slow response does not prove failure. Starting a new task can introduce a second action while the first remains unresolved.

Use the existing result or transaction reference to check progress. If the product cannot establish the status, it should say so rather than converting uncertainty into a success or failure claim.

For Solana, transaction details can be inspected through an appropriate explorer. The reference must correspond to the right network and action. See [transaction receipts](/guides/agent-transaction-receipts).

## A completed transaction is still a narrow fact

Completion can establish that the recorded action occurred. It cannot establish that the purchase was a good decision or that a token will retain value. The transaction result and the quality of the asset remain separate.

The most useful final screen leaves you with a clear account: this was the candidate, this was the permission decision, and this was the actual outcome. That lets the agent reduce your workload without making the consequence harder to understand.
