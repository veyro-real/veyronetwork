# Agentic payments, without giving up control

You should be able to tell an agent what you want and how much it can spend. “Search X for a meme coin. Spend up to $100.” That is the appeal of agentic payments: less work between an instruction and an action, with a clear boundary around your money.

An agentic payment is a payment an AI agent helps initiate or complete on someone's behalf. The agent may research an option and propose a purchase. Its permission to spend comes from the person or business behind the request. The useful question is not how independent the agent sounds. It is what the agent is actually allowed to do.

## A request is the beginning

There are two different decisions inside the $100 example. The first is what to look for. The second is how much authority to give the agent. A search can return many interesting candidates without any of them being an acceptable purchase.

Veyro's product pages describe this as giving the agent a direction while keeping the boundaries. The [Veyro demo](https://tryveyro.casa) puts an instruction and a spend limit at the center of the experience. The underlying network is Solana. You do not need to learn a new payment vocabulary to understand the idea.

The amount should be a ceiling, not a target. “Up to $100” should not mean “find a reason to spend all $100.” It should also be clear whether that number covers one action or the whole task. See [how an agent spending limit works](/guides/agent-spending-limits).

## Research, permission, result

A useful flow has three understandable stages. First, the agent looks for something that fits your request. Next, the proposed spend is checked against the permission you gave it. Finally, you receive a result that distinguishes a candidate, a blocked action, and a completed transaction.

Those stages should not blur together. Finding a token on X is not the same as buying it. An allowed action is not proof that a transaction completed. A receipt is evidence of a transaction, not proof that the token will hold its value.

Suppose the agent finds a candidate but the proposed spend exceeds your limit. A sensible result is a refusal to proceed, with a short explanation. The task did not fail merely because no money moved. Respecting the boundary is part of doing the task correctly.

## What makes this different from automatic billing?

A subscription charges under a recurring arrangement. An agent can make a choice inside a task: which candidate to investigate, which offer fits, or whether the request needs clarification. That flexibility makes clear permission more important.

You would not want a streaming subscription to choose another subscription for you. In the same way, asking an agent to research a meme coin should not quietly authorize an unrelated transfer, a larger budget, or ongoing purchases. Read [autonomous payments versus recurring payments](/guides/autonomous-payments) for a closer comparison.

## Simple does not mean guaranteed

A clear interface can reduce friction. It cannot make every asset safe or predict the market. A spending cap limits an authorized outlay; it does not stop an approved asset from losing value afterward. The [Veyro product site](https://veyro.casa) makes this distinction in its description of spending controls.

The same separation matters across the wider industry. Visa describes controls and authentication as part of its Intelligent Commerce work. That is industry context, not evidence of a Veyro card integration or partnership. [Visa Intelligent Commerce](https://www.visa.com/en-us/solutions/intelligent-commerce).

## Start with a request you can judge

Before running a task, make sure you can answer three questions: what should the agent look for, what is the maximum it may spend, and what result would count as success? If the request is ambiguous, narrowing it is more useful than adding a longer prompt full of excitement.

Afterward, look at the explanation and the actual status. A good experience lets you understand what was proposed, what was permitted, and what happened. That is the foundation of useful agentic payments: an easier action with a boundary you can still recognize.
