# Agentic commerce starts with what you want

Shopping often involves a lot of small decisions before money moves. You search, compare, check the details, and decide whether an offer fits. Agentic commerce lets an AI agent help with that work and, where you have granted permission, take an action on your behalf.

The phrase covers more than a payment. It includes discovering an option, understanding the terms, choosing a candidate, and following the outcome. A quick checkout is useful only if the agent chose something that actually matched the request.

## One instruction can contain several expectations

“Find me the best meme coin for $100” sounds straightforward. But best might mean the most discussed, the newest, the easiest to trade, or simply the most entertaining. Those are different searches. None means guaranteed profit.

The first job is to translate the request into something that can be evaluated. For a search on X, that could mean recent posts about Solana meme coins, a clear explanation for the selected candidate, and a firm spend ceiling. It should not mean treating the loudest account as an authority on where your money belongs.

The [Veyro demo](https://tryveyro.casa) makes the request and limit visible together. That is a useful expression of agentic commerce: the user's goal sits next to the user's boundary.

## Discovery and purchase are different jobs

An agent can be good at finding information without being right about a purchase. A post may use a familiar ticker while linking to a different token. A promising-looking result may lack enough evidence to act on. A price may change while the agent is researching.

The buying step therefore needs its own check. Does the proposed action still match the instruction? Is the amount within the permitted limit? Is the destination the intended one? If an important condition has changed, the agent should not rely on the fact that its earlier search looked convincing.

Our guide to [checking a meme coin candidate](/guides/checking-meme-coin-candidates) explains the practical difference between an interesting search result and an identifiable asset.

## The payment is not the whole result

An agent should give you a result you can interpret. “Done” is too vague if it could mean research finished, a candidate was selected, or a transaction was submitted. Each of those is a different outcome.

For a physical product, the eventual result includes delivery and any return. For a token transaction, it includes the correct asset, transaction status, and the amount involved. A transaction receipt can help establish what happened on the network. It cannot establish whether the purchase was a good financial decision.

Clear follow-up also makes it easier to stop. You should not have to guess whether an agent is still searching, waiting for permission, or acting under an existing allowance. See [reading an agent's result](/guides/understanding-agent-results).

## Where the wider industry fits

Visa and Mastercard are both working on commerce involving AI agents. Mastercard's European discussion of Agent Pay describes controlled, consent-based purchasing scenarios and pilots. This is evidence of a broader direction in payments, not a claim that every merchant or agent already supports the same experience. [Mastercard on trusted agentic commerce](https://newsroom.mastercard.com/news/europe/en/perspectives/en/2026/europe-is-building-the-foundations-for-trusted-agentic-commerce/).

Veyro's public experience is centered on Solana. Reading about card networks can help explain the category without suggesting that Veyro has a partnership or card-payment feature it has not announced.

## What to look for as a user

Start with the parts you can see: a specific instruction, a visible limit, a reason for the candidate, and an understandable outcome. Be cautious about interfaces that make a confident recommendation but hide the amount or skip straight from a search result to a success message.

Agentic commerce should reduce the work of reaching a decision. It should also preserve the information you need to judge that decision. The most useful experience is one where the agent does more of the searching while you still recognize the terms under which it can act.
